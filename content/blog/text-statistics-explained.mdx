Text statistics are the measurable properties of a piece of writing, such as word count, sentence count, average sentence length, average word length, unique words and estimated reading time. Together they give you an objective snapshot of how long, dense and varied your text is. Editors use them to spot problems that are hard to see by reading alone.

The free [RankBoost Text Statistics](/tools/text-statistics) tool calculates all of these metrics at once in a single dashboard.

## Why text statistics are useful

Reading your own draft is a poor way to judge it. You know what you meant, so you skim past long sentences and repeated words. Numbers do not have that blind spot. Text statistics help you:

- Confirm that a draft fits a length brief
- Catch dense passages before readers do
- Compare drafts to see whether edits improved clarity
- Keep a consistent style across multiple writers or articles

Statistics do not tell you whether writing is good. They tell you where to look.

## The metrics, explained

Here is every metric the RankBoost tool reports, and what each one means.

### Length metrics

- **Words:** total word count. Hyphenated words and contractions count as one.
- **Characters** and **Chars (no spaces):** total length, with and without whitespace.
- **Sentences:** text split at periods, question marks and exclamation points.
- **Paragraphs:** blocks of text separated by a blank line.
- **Lines:** every line, including blank ones, split at line breaks.
- **Estimated pages:** words divided by roughly 500 per page. Real page counts depend on font, spacing and margins.

### Complexity metrics

- **Avg word length:** the average number of characters per word. Higher values often mean more technical or formal vocabulary.
- **Avg words / sentence:** the average sentence length, one of the strongest signals of how easy text is to read.
- **Syllables:** an estimated total, calculated with a common heuristic for English. It feeds readability formulas, and may be slightly off for unusual words.
- **Longest word:** handy for spotting jargon or awkward compounds.

### Vocabulary metrics

- **Unique words:** the number of distinct words, ignoring capitalization. Comparing this to total words gives a rough sense of vocabulary variety.

### Time metrics

- **Reading time:** based on an average silent reading speed of 225 words per minute.
- **Speaking time:** based on a comfortable speaking pace of 130 words per minute.

## How to interpret the numbers

The ranges below are general guidelines for web content aimed at a broad audience. Expert or academic writing can reasonably sit higher.

| Metric | Comfortable range for web content | What a high value may mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Avg words per sentence | Around 15 to 20 | Long, dense sentences |
| Avg word length | Around 4 to 5 characters | Technical or formal vocabulary |
| Sentences per paragraph | Around 2 to 4 | Walls of text |

The unique word ratio needs context. Short texts naturally have a high share of unique words, while long texts repeat common words more often. Compare drafts of similar length rather than chasing a fixed percentage.

## Turning statistics into edits

### Long average sentences

Break long sentences at conjunctions and give each idea its own sentence. Aim for variety, not uniformity.

### High average word length

Swap long words for plain alternatives where meaning allows: "use" instead of "utilize", "help" instead of "facilitate".

### Low vocabulary variety

Find the words you lean on and vary them. The [Word Counter](/tools/word-counter) shows your most frequent words in a chart, which makes overuse obvious.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating guidelines as rules.** A legal brief and a product page should not hit the same numbers.
- **Analyzing the wrong text.** Strip out navigation, captions and code samples, which skew averages.
- **Optimizing one number in isolation.** Cutting every sentence to eight words may lower your average but hurt flow.

## FAQ

### What is a good unique word ratio?

There is no universal target, because the ratio drops naturally as text gets longer. Use it to compare versions of the same piece or articles of similar length.

### Why are there more lines than paragraphs?

Lines count every line break, including blank lines between paragraphs and single breaks inside them. Paragraphs only count blocks separated by blank lines.

### Are syllable counts exact?

No. Syllables are estimated with a rule-based heuristic for English. It is accurate enough for readability analysis but may miscount some unusual words or names.

### Can text statistics detect AI-written text?

No. These metrics describe length, structure and vocabulary. They are not designed to identify who or what wrote a text.
