The Flesch Reading Ease score rates how easy a piece of English text is to read on a scale from 0 to 100, where higher numbers mean easier text. It's calculated from just two things: average sentence length and average syllables per word. For most web content, a score of 60 or higher is a sensible target.

## What the Flesch Reading Ease score measures

Rudolf Flesch designed the formula in the 1940s to estimate how hard a text is to read without needing a human tester. It doesn't judge grammar, accuracy or tone. It only looks at structure:

- **Words per sentence** — longer sentences demand more working memory.
- **Syllables per word** — longer words tend to be less familiar and slower to process.

Because it's purely mechanical, the score is a quick diagnostic rather than a verdict on quality. A clear, well-argued article can still score low if it relies on necessary technical terms.

## The Flesch Reading Ease formula

The standard formula is:

**Score = 206.835 − (1.015 × words per sentence) − (84.6 × syllables per word)**

The syllable term carries a much heavier weight, so word choice usually moves the score more than sentence length does. Here's how two hypothetical drafts compare:

| Draft | Words per sentence | Syllables per word | Approximate score |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Plain blog post | 15 | 1.5 | 64.7 |
| Dense report | 25 | 1.7 | 37.6 |

Shaving a fraction of a syllable off the average word can lift the score by 10 points or more.

## How to interpret your score

The RankBoost tool labels each score with a band so you don't have to memorize the ranges:

| Score | Band | Typical fit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 90–100 | Very easy | Children's content, simple instructions |
| 80–89 | Easy | Conversational marketing copy |
| 70–79 | Fairly easy | Friendly blog posts |
| 60–69 | Standard | Most web pages and news articles |
| 50–59 | Fairly difficult | In-depth guides, B2B content |
| 30–49 | Difficult | Academic or technical writing |
| 0–29 | Very confusing | Legal and specialist documents |

So what is a good Flesch Reading Ease score? For a general audience, aim for the Standard band or above. Specialist audiences can tolerate lower scores, but you should drop there on purpose, not by accident.

## Flesch Reading Ease vs Flesch-Kincaid grade level

The two are easy to confuse. Both use the same inputs, but they report in opposite directions:

- **Flesch Reading Ease** gives a 0–100 score where higher is easier.
- **Flesch-Kincaid Grade Level** gives a US school grade where lower is easier.

A Flesch Reading Ease of about 65 typically lines up with a Flesch-Kincaid grade of around 8. If you'd rather think in grade levels, the [Reading Grade Level tool](/tools/reading-grade) gives you a single consensus grade.

## How to raise a low readability score

### Swap long words for short ones

Since syllables carry the most weight, start here. "Use" instead of "utilize", "help" instead of "facilitate", "about" instead of "approximately". The [Complex Word Finder](/tools/complex-word-finder) lists every three-plus-syllable word in your text so you can decide which to simplify.

### Split your longest sentences

Look for sentences joined with "and", "which" or "because" that could become two. The [Long Sentence Detector](/tools/long-sentence-detector) flags anything over your chosen word limit, longest first.

### Cut filler

Words like "really", "basically" and "in order to" add length without meaning. Removing them shortens sentences and often improves the score.

### Keep necessary jargon, explain it once

If your audience needs a technical term, keep it. Define it the first time, then use it consistently rather than piling on synonyms.

## Common mistakes

- **Chasing 100.** Extremely high scores can read as choppy or childish. Match the audience instead.
- **Testing tiny samples.** A two-sentence sample can swing wildly. Test at least a few paragraphs.
- **Ignoring meaning.** You can game the formula with short words and fragments and still write something confusing. Always reread for sense.
- **Scoring lists and code.** Bullet lists without punctuation and code snippets distort sentence counts. Score your prose sections.

## FAQ

### Does the Flesch Reading Ease score affect Google rankings?

Google hasn't said it uses readability formulas as a ranking factor. Clearer content does tend to keep readers engaged, though, which supports the helpful, people-first content search engines say they reward.

### Why does my score differ between tools?

Tools count syllables and split sentences slightly differently, especially around abbreviations, numbers and headings. Small differences are normal. Use one tool consistently to track changes over time.

### Can the score be negative or above 100?

The raw formula can go below 0 or above 100 for extreme text. The RankBoost tool clamps the result to the 0–100 range so it stays easy to interpret.

### Does the formula work for languages other than English?

The standard constants were built for English. Other languages have adapted versions, so treat scores on non-English text as rough at best.
