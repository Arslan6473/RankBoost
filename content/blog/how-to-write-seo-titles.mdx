To write SEO titles that work, put your primary keyword near the start, keep the title to roughly 50–60 characters (about 600 pixels) so it isn't cut off, and give searchers a concrete reason to click, such as a number, a benefit or a clear promise. A good title describes the page accurately first and persuades second.

Below is a practical process for writing and testing titles, using the free [SEO Title Analyzer](/tools/seo-title-analyzer) to score each draft.

## Why SEO titles matter

The title tag is one of the clearest signals you give search engines about a page's topic, and it is usually the headline people see in search results. A strong title helps in two ways:

- **Relevance:** it tells search engines and users what the page covers.
- **Click-through:** it competes for attention with every other result on the page.

Google may rewrite titles it considers inaccurate, stuffed or too long, so a well-crafted title also makes it more likely your version is the one displayed.

## The ideal SEO title length

The commonly recommended range is about 50–60 characters. The real limit is visual: Google truncates titles based on pixel width, roughly 600 pixels on desktop. Wide letters like "M" and "W" use more space than narrow ones like "i" and "l", so two titles with the same character count can display differently.

That's why it helps to check both numbers. The [SEO Title Analyzer](/tools/seo-title-analyzer) reports character length and an estimated pixel width, flagging anything over 600 pixels.

## How to write an SEO title step by step

### Step 1: Start with the primary keyword

Place the main keyword phrase in the first half of the title where possible. Readers scan the beginning of each result, and front-loading the topic makes relevance obvious.

### Step 2: Add a reason to click

Specificity earns clicks. Options include:

- A number: "7 Ways", "in 10 Minutes", "2026"
- A benefit: "Save Hours", "Without Code"
- A format signal: "Guide", "Checklist", "Template"

### Step 3: Use one or two power words

Power words are emotionally or practically compelling terms like "best", "proven", "simple", "complete" or "ultimate". Used sparingly, they make a headline feel more useful. Used heavily, they read as clickbait.

### Step 4: Score the draft

Paste your title into the analyzer. It reads the first line you enter and returns a score out of 100, graded Strong, Okay or Weak, based on:

- Whether the length falls in the 30–60 character range
- Whether the estimated width is 600 pixels or less
- Whether the title contains a number
- How many power words it uses (they are listed so you can see which ones counted)

It also shows the word count. Treat the score as a checklist, not a verdict: a title can score lower and still be the right choice if it's the most accurate description of the page.

### Step 5: Preview it in context

Once a title scores well, see how it looks alongside a description with the [SERP Preview](/tools/serp-preview) tool. If you need ideas to start from, the [Meta Title Generator](/tools/meta-title-generator) can produce variations.

## Common title tag mistakes

- **Duplicate titles across pages.** Each page should have a unique title that reflects its own content.
- **Clickbait that the page doesn't deliver.** Misleading titles can raise bounce rates and may be rewritten.
- **Stuffing several keyword variants.** One clear phrase beats three awkward ones.
- **Leaving the brand to chance.** If you include your brand, put it at the end, separated by a dash or pipe, so it doesn't push the keyword out of view.
- **Ignoring the H1.** Your on-page headline doesn't have to match the title exactly, but they should describe the same topic. The [on-page SEO guide](/blog/on-page-seo-guide) covers how these elements work together.

## FAQ

### Should the SEO title and H1 be the same?

They can be, but they don't have to be. Many sites use a shorter, keyword-focused title tag and a more conversational H1. What matters is that both clearly describe the same page.

### Is it bad if my title is under 30 characters?

Not necessarily, especially for brand or home pages. Short titles often waste space you could use for a benefit or qualifier, though, so consider whether adding detail would help searchers.

### Do capital letters affect SEO titles?

Capitalization doesn't affect rankings. Title case and sentence case are both fine; choose one style and use it consistently. Note that capitals are wider, so all-caps titles truncate sooner.

### How often should I test new titles?

Revisit titles for important pages when their click-through rate in Google Search Console looks low compared with their position. Change one thing at a time so you can tell what made the difference.
