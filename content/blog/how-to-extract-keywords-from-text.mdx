To extract keywords from text, you remove common filler words, count how often the remaining words and short phrases appear, and rank them by frequency. The terms at the top of that list are the topics the text emphasizes most. A keyword extractor does this automatically, so you can paste in any article and see its main themes in seconds.

This guide explains how keyword extraction works, how to use the free [Keyword Extractor](/tools/keyword-extractor), and what to do with the results.

## What keyword extraction is

Keyword extraction is the process of identifying the most important words and phrases in a body of text. Instead of reading a 2,000-word page and guessing its focus, you get a ranked list of the terms it relies on.

It answers a different question from keyword density. Density asks "how often does my chosen keyword appear?", which is covered in our guide to [keyword density](/blog/what-is-keyword-density). Extraction asks "which keywords does this text actually emphasize?", without you naming a keyword first. That makes it useful when you're analyzing content you didn't write, or checking whether your own draft drifted off topic.

## Why extracting keywords is useful

- **Confirm topical focus.** If your target topic doesn't appear near the top of the list, the page may not be sending a clear signal.
- **Analyze competitor pages.** Paste the body copy of a top-ranking page to see which concepts it covers repeatedly.
- **Find heading and internal link ideas.** Frequent phrases often deserve their own subheading or a link to a dedicated page.
- **Summarize documents quickly.** Extracted terms give a fast sense of what a report, transcript or brief is about.
- **Tag and categorize content.** The top terms make sensible starting points for tags, categories or metadata.

## How to extract keywords step by step

### Step 1: Prepare the text

Copy only the main content: the body of the article, without navigation menus, footers, cookie notices or sidebars. Boilerplate adds noise that can push real topics down the list.

### Step 2: Paste it into the extractor

Open the [Keyword Extractor](/tools/keyword-extractor) and paste the text. Everything runs in your browser, so you don't need an account.

### Step 3: Read the top keywords

The tool removes common stop words, such as "the", "and", "with" and "your", and ignores very short words of two letters or fewer. It then shows up to 10 top single-word keywords with their counts, plus a bar chart so you can compare them at a glance.

### Step 4: Check the key phrases

Below the single words, you'll see up to eight two-word key phrases that appear more than once, such as "content marketing". Phrases are often more meaningful than single words, because "marketing" alone could mean many things.

### Step 5: Compare against your goal

Ask whether the extracted list matches what the page is meant to rank for. If it doesn't, you know what to adjust.

## Example: extracting keywords from a short paragraph

Take this sample text:

> Content marketing is a strategic approach focused on creating valuable content. A good content marketing strategy attracts and retains a clearly defined audience. Content marketing builds trust and drives profitable customer action.

| Result | What it tells you |

Words like "strategy", "audience" and "trust" each appear once, which hints at subtopics the paragraph mentions but doesn't develop.

## How to use extracted keywords

### Tighten off-topic drafts

If secondary words outrank your main topic, cut tangents or expand the sections that matter.

### Spot gaps against competitors

Extract keywords from two or three ranking pages and from your own. Concepts that appear on every competitor page but not yours may be gaps worth covering, as long as they genuinely help the reader.

### Plan clusters and intent

Feed the phrases you find into the [Keyword Cluster Generator](/tools/keyword-cluster-generator) to group them into topics, or into the [Search Intent Analyzer](/tools/search-intent-analyzer) to see which content format each one suits.

## Common mistakes

- **Including boilerplate text.** Menus and footers distort the counts.
- **Chasing every extracted term.** The list describes what's there; it isn't a checklist of words to add.
- **Ignoring phrases.** Single words are broad. Two-word phrases usually reveal the real topic.
- **Treating frequency as quality.** A word appearing often means it's emphasized, not that it's well covered.

## FAQ

### Can I extract keywords from a competitor's website?

Yes. Copy the visible body text of their page and paste it into the extractor. You're analyzing publicly visible content, so focus on what topics they cover rather than copying their wording.

### Why are some obvious words missing from the results?

Common function words are filtered out on purpose, and words of two letters or fewer are skipped. That's why acronyms like "AI" or "UX" won't appear as single keywords.

### How much text do I need for useful results?

A few paragraphs is enough to see a pattern, but full articles give more reliable phrase results, since phrases only appear when they repeat.

### Is keyword extraction the same as keyword research?

No. Keyword research looks at what people search for, usually with search volume data. Extraction analyzes text you already have. They work well together: research tells you what to target, extraction tells you whether your content reflects it.
