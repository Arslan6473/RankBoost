To simplify text, replace long or formal words with everyday ones, cut phrases that add words but no meaning, and break long sentences into shorter ones. For example, "utilize" becomes "use," "in order to" becomes "to," and "approximately" becomes "about." Simplified text keeps the same meaning but asks less effort from the reader.

Plain language is not about writing for children. It is about removing the friction between your reader and your message. Busy people skim, readers in a second language translate, and almost everyone is reading on a small screen. Simple text works better for all of them.

## Why simplifying text matters

Dense, jargon-heavy writing creates three problems. Readers take longer to understand it, they are more likely to misunderstand it, and they are more likely to leave. For web content, that means fewer people reach your key point or your call to action.

Simplifying also helps you as a writer. When you force a sentence into plain words, you often discover that it was vague, or that it was hiding two separate ideas. Our [readability guide](/blog/readability-guide) covers how readability scores measure this.

## Plain language techniques that work

**Choose the everyday word.** Beyond the swaps above, try "start" for "commence," "buy" for "purchase," "show" for "demonstrate" and "enough" for "sufficient."

**Cut empty phrases.** "Due to the fact that" means "because." "At this point in time" means "now." "In the event that" means "if."

**One idea per sentence.** If a sentence contains "and" twice, it probably wants to be two sentences. The [Long Sentence Detector](/tools/long-sentence-detector) helps you find candidates.

**Prefer active voice.** "The team approved the plan" is clearer than "The plan was approved by the team."

**Turn nouns back into verbs.** "Make a decision" becomes "decide." "Conduct an analysis" becomes "analyze."

**Explain necessary jargon.** Some technical terms cannot be avoided. Define them the first time they appear.

## Common mistakes when simplifying

- **Losing precision.** Simple does not mean vague. Keep the specific terms your reader actually needs.
- **Over-shortening.** A string of very short sentences can feel choppy. Vary length a little.
- **Being condescending.** Plain language respects the reader's time. It should never sound like you think they cannot understand.
- **Skipping the meaning check.** Every swap should keep the original meaning. Reread before you publish.

## FAQ

### Is simplified text bad for SEO?

No. Simple, clear text is easier for readers to engage with, and search engines aim to reward content that satisfies people. You can still use your target keywords naturally in plain language.

### What reading level should I aim for?

For most general web content, a grade 6 to 8 reading level is a sensible goal. Specialist content for experts can be higher, but clarity still helps them read faster.

### Should I simplify technical documentation?

Yes, carefully. Keep the technical terms readers need, but simplify everything around them: connecting phrases, instructions and explanations.

### How is simplifying different from summarizing?

Simplifying keeps all of the content but makes it easier to read. Summarizing removes content to keep only the main points. If you need a shorter version, the [AI Summarizer](/tools/ai-summarizer) is the better fit.
