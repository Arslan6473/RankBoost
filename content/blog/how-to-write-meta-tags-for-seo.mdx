To write meta tags for SEO, create a unique title tag of roughly 50 to 60 characters with your main keyword near the start, and a meta description of roughly 150 to 160 characters that summarizes the page and gives searchers a reason to click. Both should accurately describe the page's content. Search engines may rewrite them, but well-written tags are more likely to be shown as you wrote them.

Meta tags are small pieces of HTML that describe a page to search engines and browsers. Of the many meta tags that exist, two matter most for search visibility: the title tag and the meta description. Together they form the snippet that people see in search results, so they directly shape whether someone clicks.

## What the title tag and meta description do

**The title tag** is the clickable headline in search results and the text shown in the browser tab. It is a strong signal of what the page is about, which is why keyword placement matters here.

**The meta description** is the short summary underneath. It is not considered a direct ranking factor, but it influences click-through rate, and search engines often bold words that match the searcher's query.

Our [on-page SEO guide](/blog/on-page-seo-guide) explains how these fit with headings, content and internal links.

## Recommended lengths

| Tag | Common recommendation | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title tag | About 50 to 60 characters | Longer titles are more likely to be truncated |
| Meta description | About 150 to 160 characters | Longer descriptions are often cut off with an ellipsis |

Search results are actually limited by pixel width rather than character count, so treat these as guides, not hard rules. A preview tool such as the [SERP Preview](/tools/serp-preview) shows how your snippet is likely to look.

## Step by step: writing meta tags by hand

**1. Identify the primary keyword.** One per page. Put it near the start of the title.

**2. Write the title as a promise.** Say what the page delivers: "How to Repot a Snake Plant in 5 Steps" is clearer than "Snake Plant Care."

**3. Add your brand if there is room.** Many sites append " | Brand" at the end. Drop it if it pushes the title past the limit.

**4. Write the description as an answer.** Summarize the value in one or two sentences, include the keyword once, and add a light call to action like "Learn how" or "See examples."

**5. Make every page unique.** Duplicate titles and descriptions across pages make it harder for search engines and users to tell them apart.

**6. Preview and test.** Check length with a preview tool, then watch click-through rates in your search console data over time.

## Meta tag examples

Weak:

```
Title: Home
Description: Welcome to our website. We sell lots of things.
```

Stronger:

```
Title: Handmade Ceramic Mugs | Small-Batch Pottery Studio
Description: Shop handmade ceramic mugs thrown and glazed in small batches.
Dishwasher safe, gift ready and shipped within three days.
```

The stronger version names the product, describes what makes it different and gives a practical reason to click.

## Common meta tag mistakes

- **Leaving them blank.** Search engines will generate a snippet, but it may not be the one you want.
- **Keyword stuffing.** Repeating the keyword in the title or description looks spammy and wastes space.
- **Misleading copy.** A description that does not match the page leads to quick exits.
- **Using the same tags everywhere.** Template defaults copied across hundreds of pages give search engines little to work with.
- **Forgetting to update.** When a page changes significantly, revisit its tags.

## FAQ

### Do meta keywords still matter?

The meta keywords tag is widely reported to be ignored by major search engines for ranking. Focus your effort on the title tag and meta description instead.

### Why does Google show a different description than mine?

Search engines sometimes rewrite snippets to better match a specific query. Writing a clear, accurate description that matches the page's main topic improves the chances yours is used.

### Should the title tag match the H1?

It does not have to match exactly. The two should describe the same topic, but the title tag can be tighter for search results while the H1 is written for readers on the page.

### How often should I update meta tags?

Review them when you update the page content, change its target keyword or notice a low click-through rate for a page that ranks well.
