Readability measures how easy your text is to understand. It's one of the most overlooked factors in content performance — because if people can't easily read your writing, they leave.

## How readability scores work

Most readability formulas look at two things: **sentence length** and **word complexity**. Longer sentences and more multi-syllable words push the reading level up.

The most popular formula, **Flesch Reading Ease**, produces a score from 0–100. Higher is easier:

- **90–100** — Very easy (5th grade)
- **60–70** — Standard (8th–9th grade)
- **30–50** — Difficult (college)
- **0–30** — Very confusing (graduate)

You can measure all of this with our [Readability Score](/tools/readability-score) tool.

## What reading level should you target?

For most web content, aim for a **grade 6–8** reading level. That's the comfortable reading level of the average adult — and it maximizes comprehension and reach. Check yours with the [Reading Grade](/tools/reading-grade) tool.

## Techniques to improve readability

### Shorten your sentences

Aim for an average of 15–20 words per sentence. Break long sentences into two. Find the worst offenders with the [Long Sentence Detector](/tools/long-sentence-detector).

### Simplify your words

Prefer "use" over "utilize," "help" over "facilitate." The [Complex Word Finder](/tools/complex-word-finder) highlights words worth swapping.

### Prefer active voice

"We shipped the feature" beats "The feature was shipped by us." Catch passive constructions with the [Passive Voice Checker](/tools/passive-voice-checker).

### Add transitions

Words like "however," "therefore" and "for example" guide readers through your argument. The [Transition Word Checker](/tools/transition-word-checker) shows how often you use them.

## Readability and SEO

Readable content keeps people on the page longer and reduces bounce rate — both signals that correlate with better rankings. Clear writing isn't just good for readers; it's good for search too.
