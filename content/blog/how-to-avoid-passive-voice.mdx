To avoid passive voice, find sentences where the subject receives the action ("The report was written by Sam") and rewrite them so the subject performs it ("Sam wrote the report"). The fastest way to spot them is to look for a form of "to be" followed by a past participle. Most writers aim to keep passive voice to about 10% of their sentences or less.

## What is passive voice?

In an active sentence, the subject does the action:

- **Active:** The team launched the new site.

In a passive sentence, the subject has the action done to it, and the doer either moves to the end or disappears:

- **Passive:** The new site was launched by the team.
- **Passive, no doer:** The new site was launched.

Passive voice isn't a grammar error. It's a style choice. The problem is that it often creeps in by accident and makes writing longer, vaguer and harder to follow.

## Why passive voice hurts readability

- **It adds words.** "Mistakes were made by the team" is longer than "The team made mistakes."
- **It hides responsibility.** Readers can't tell who did what, which matters in instructions, reports and policies.
- **It slows readers down.** People have to hold the action in mind before they find out who did it.

Clear, direct sentences help readers stay with your content, which is good for them and good for your pages. Our [readability guide](/blog/readability-guide) covers more techniques like this.

## How to spot passive voice

Look for this pattern: a form of "to be" (am, is, are, was, were, be, been, being) followed by a past participle (usually a verb ending in -ed, or an irregular form such as "written", "taken" or "made").

| Passive | Active rewrite |
| --- | --- |
| The email was sent by our support team. | Our support team sent the email. |
| Your password is being reset. | We're resetting your password. |
| The results were carefully reviewed. | Our analysts carefully reviewed the results. |
| A decision has been made. | The board made a decision. |
| Cookies are used to improve the site. | We use cookies to improve the site. |

A handy test: if you can add "by zombies" after the verb and the sentence still makes sense, it's probably passive. "The email was sent by zombies" works, so it's passive.

## Limits of automated passive voice detection

Because it relies on patterns, any passive voice detector can flag some false positives and miss some real cases:

- **Adjectives that look like participles.** "She was tired" or "I am interested" match the pattern but describe a state, not an action. Leave them alone.
- **"Get" passives.** "He got promoted" is passive in meaning but doesn't use a "to be" verb, so it may not be flagged.
- **Unusual irregular verbs.** Less common participles may not be recognized.

Read each flagged sentence and use your judgment.

## How to rewrite passive voice step by step

1. **Find the doer.** Ask "who or what performed this action?"
2. **Make the doer the subject.** Move it to the front of the sentence.
3. **Use an active verb.** Drop the "to be" helper and pick a direct verb.
4. **Add a doer if it's missing.** If the sentence says "the change was made", decide who made it: "we", "the editor", "the system".

## When passive voice is the right choice

Don't remove every passive sentence. Passive voice is useful when:

- **The doer is unknown.** "The window was broken overnight."
- **The doer doesn't matter.** "The site is updated daily."
- **You want to emphasize the receiver.** "Three new tools were added to RankBoost this month."
- **The convention calls for it.** Scientific methods sections often use passive voice by convention.

The goal is to use passive voice deliberately, not by habit.

## FAQ

### What percentage of passive voice is acceptable?

A common guideline is 10% of sentences or less. Technical and scientific writing often runs higher, and that's fine if each passive sentence is a deliberate choice.

### Is "is being" always passive?

Not always. "He is being careful" uses "being" with an adjective, which is not passive. "The site is being rebuilt" is passive because "rebuilt" is a participle describing an action done to the site.

### Does passive voice affect SEO?

Search engines don't penalize passive voice directly. However, overusing it makes content wordier and harder to read, and readable content tends to serve visitors better.

### Is passive voice grammatically wrong?

No. Passive voice is grammatically correct. It's a matter of clarity and style, not correctness.
