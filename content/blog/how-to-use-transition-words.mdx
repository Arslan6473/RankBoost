Transition words are words and phrases like "however", "therefore" and "for example" that show how one idea relates to the next. To use them well, pick the transition that matches the relationship you mean (contrast, cause, sequence or example) and place it where readers would otherwise have to guess the connection. A common guideline is to include a transition in roughly 30% of your sentences.

You can measure how often you use them with the free [Transition Word Checker](/tools/transition-word-checker). It shows the percentage of sentences that contain a transition word and lists which ones you used, and how often.

## Why transition words matter

Without transitions, paragraphs can read like a list of disconnected facts. Readers have to work out for themselves whether the next sentence adds to, contrasts with or results from the previous one. Transitions do that work for them.

Good transitions:

- **Signal direction**, so readers know whether to expect agreement, contrast or a conclusion.
- **Make arguments easier to follow**, especially in longer guides and essays.
- **Smooth out short sentences**, so a page of crisp sentences doesn't feel choppy.

## Transition words list by type

The right word depends on the relationship you want to show:

| Relationship | Transition words |
| --- | --- |
| Adding information | also, additionally, furthermore, moreover, in addition, besides |
| Contrast | however, in contrast, on the other hand, conversely, whereas, nevertheless |
| Cause and effect | therefore, consequently, as a result, hence, thus, accordingly |
| Sequence and time | first, next, then, meanwhile, subsequently, finally, afterward |
| Examples | for example, for instance, specifically, namely |
| Comparison | similarly, likewise |
| Emphasis | indeed, in fact |
| Conclusion | in conclusion, ultimately |

All of the words above are among the transitions the RankBoost checker recognizes.

## Transition words examples

Here's how adding transitions changes a short passage.

**Without transitions:**

The new homepage loads quickly. Visitors stay longer. Sign-ups have grown. The checkout page is still slow.

**With transitions:**

The new homepage loads quickly. As a result, visitors stay longer, and sign-ups have grown. However, the checkout page is still slow.

The second version uses the same facts, but readers can now see how they connect.

## Best practices for using transition words

### Match the transition to the logic

"Therefore" means the next point follows from the last. If it doesn't, the word confuses rather than helps. Choose based on meaning, not variety.

### Vary your choices

Leaning on the same two or three transitions makes writing feel repetitive. The checker's frequency list shows which ones you rely on most.

### Put transitions where the shift happens

Transitions usually work best at the start of a sentence or paragraph, where the change in direction begins.

### Don't force them

Hitting a percentage is not the goal. A sentence that already follows naturally doesn't need "furthermore" in front of it. Use the 30% guideline as a prompt to review, not a quota to fill.

## Common mistakes

- **Stacking transitions.** "However, on the other hand, nevertheless" in the same paragraph is too much.
- **Using formal transitions everywhere.** "Moreover" and "henceforth" suit academic writing. In a casual blog post, "also" and "so" often read better.
- **Wrong relationship.** Using "for example" before something that isn't an example, or "as a result" before something that isn't a result.
- **Treating the count as a quality check.** The tool counts words, not meaning. Reread your text to make sure each transition makes sense.

## FAQ

### What's the difference between transition words and conjunctions?

Conjunctions like "and", "but" and "because" join parts of a sentence. Transition words often link whole sentences or paragraphs. The two overlap, and both help flow.

### Should every paragraph start with a transition?

No. Starting every paragraph the same way becomes repetitive. Use a transition when the relationship to the previous paragraph isn't already obvious.

### Are transition words good for essays and blog posts alike?

Yes, but the style differs. Essays often use more formal transitions such as "consequently" and "furthermore". Blog posts usually work better with simpler ones such as "so", "also" and "but".

### Can too many transition words hurt readability?

Yes. Overused transitions add length and can make writing sound stiff. If your percentage is very high, check whether every transition is doing real work.
