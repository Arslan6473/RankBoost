## What paraphrasing is (and isn't)

Paraphrasing means expressing the same idea in different words. It differs from:

- **Summarizing**, which shortens text to its main points.
- **Quoting**, which reproduces the exact words in quotation marks.
- **Simplifying**, which specifically lowers the reading level.

Good reasons to paraphrase include tightening a wordy draft, avoiding the same phrasing appearing across several pages, adapting your own older content, and breaking through writer's block when a sentence just won't come out right.

In both cases, the meaning stays the same but filler words such as "really", "quite", "basically" and "actually" are gone. Tightening wordy prose like this is one of the most practical uses of a rewriter.

## How to paraphrase without plagiarizing

Paraphrasing someone else's work doesn't make it yours. To stay on the right side of plagiarism rules and search engine quality guidelines:

- **Credit the source** when you paraphrase someone else's ideas, research or data.
- **Add your own value.** Combine the paraphrased point with your own analysis, examples or experience.
- **Don't spin whole articles.** Running a competitor's post through a rewriter and publishing it is likely to produce thin, unoriginal content, which search engines aim to filter out.
- **Follow the rules where you are.** Schools, publishers and clients may have their own policies on AI-assisted writing.

## Best practices for AI paraphrasing

### Check meaning before style

The most common problem with any paraphrase is a subtle shift in meaning. "May reduce" becoming "reduces", for example, turns a cautious claim into a firm one. Check accuracy first, then polish.

### Keep key terms intact

If you're writing for SEO, make sure your target keyword and important technical terms survive the rewrite. Put them back if they were lost.

### Read it aloud

Reading the rewrite aloud is a quick way to catch awkward phrasing or rhythm.

## Common mistakes

- **Rewriting too much at once.** Long inputs make it harder to spot changes in meaning.
- **Accepting the first output.** Treat the rewrite as a draft and edit it.
- **Losing important qualifiers.** Words like "usually", "may" and "some" often matter.
- **Forgetting attribution.** Rephrasing a source doesn't remove the need to credit it.

## FAQ

### Is AI paraphrasing the same as plagiarism?

Not automatically. Paraphrasing your own content, or paraphrasing a source and crediting it, is generally acceptable. Passing off someone else's ideas as your own is plagiarism, whether you rephrase them by hand or with AI.

### Will paraphrased content rank in search?

Search engines aim to reward helpful, original content. A paraphrase that adds nothing new to the original is unlikely to stand out. Use paraphrasing to improve your own writing, not to copy someone else's.

### How do I keep my brand voice when using a rewriter?

Edit the output with your style guide in mind. Swap generic words for the terms your brand uses, and adjust sentence length to match your usual style.

### Should I paraphrase quotes?

No. If you're using someone's exact words, quote them directly and attribute them. Paraphrase only when you're explaining an idea in your own words.
