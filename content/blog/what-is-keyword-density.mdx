Keyword density is the percentage of times a keyword appears in your content relative to the total number of words. It's one of the oldest metrics in SEO — and while it's no longer a direct ranking factor, it's still a useful signal of topical focus.

## How to calculate keyword density

The formula is simple:

**Keyword density = (keyword occurrences ÷ total words) × 100**

If your 1,000-word article uses the phrase "running shoes" 15 times, your density is 1.5%. You can measure this instantly with our [Keyword Density Checker](/tools/keyword-density).

## What is a good keyword density?

There's no magic number, but a healthy range for your primary keyword is **1–2%**. Going much higher risks "keyword stuffing" — cramming keywords unnaturally, which hurts readability and can trigger spam filters.

Instead of obsessing over a target percentage, focus on:

- Using your keyword naturally in the first 100 words
- Including it in at least one heading
- Adding semantic variations and related terms

## Why keyword stuffing backfires

Search engines have moved far beyond counting keywords. Modern algorithms understand meaning, context and intent. Stuffing keywords makes content awkward to read, increases bounce rate, and signals low quality.

> Write for humans first. If your content reads naturally and covers the topic thoroughly, your keyword density usually takes care of itself.

## The modern approach

Rather than chasing density, aim for **topical coverage**. Use tools like our [Keyword Extractor](/tools/keyword-extractor) to see which terms your content emphasizes, and compare that to what your target audience is actually searching for.

Keyword density is a helpful diagnostic — a way to catch overuse or underuse — but it's a starting point, not a goal.
