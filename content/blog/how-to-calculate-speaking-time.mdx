To calculate speaking time, divide your script's word count by your speaking pace in words per minute. A comfortable presentation pace is around 130 words per minute, so a 650-word speech takes about five minutes to deliver. Use a slower pace for formal or emotional speeches and a faster one for casual, conversational delivery.

## The speaking time formula

**Speaking time in minutes = total words ÷ words per minute**

People speak much more slowly than they read silently, so the words-per-minute figure for speech is lower than for reading. Here is how common word counts translate at different paces:

| Word count | Slow (100 WPM) | Comfortable (130 WPM) | Brisk (160 WPM) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 300 words | 3 min | 2.3 min | 1.9 min |
| 650 words | 6.5 min | 5 min | 4.1 min |
| 1,300 words | 13 min | 10 min | 8.1 min |
| 2,600 words | 26 min | 20 min | 16.3 min |

## How many words for common speech lengths

Working backwards is often more useful. Multiply your time slot by your pace to get a target word count. At about 130 words per minute:

- **1-minute elevator pitch:** about 130 words
- **3-minute toast or update:** about 390 words
- **5-minute speech:** about 650 words
- **10-minute presentation:** about 1,300 words
- **20-minute talk:** about 2,600 words

If you plan to pause for slides, questions, applause or demonstrations, write fewer words than the target.

## Choosing your speaking pace

There is no single correct pace. Your words per minute depend on your style, the setting and the content.

- **Slow and deliberate, around 100 WPM:** formal speeches, eulogies, content with complex ideas, or audiences listening in a second language.
- **Comfortable, around 130 WPM:** most presentations, webinars and narrated videos.
- **Brisk, 150 WPM and above:** energetic conversational delivery, podcasts and casual explainer videos.

The most accurate way to find your pace is to time yourself. Read a 260-word passage aloud at your natural speed. If it takes two minutes, your pace is 130 WPM. Enter that number into the calculator for future scripts.

## Best practices for timing a speech

### Build in a buffer

Real delivery includes pauses, emphasis, audience reactions and the odd stumble. Many speakers aim to finish their written script at around 80 to 90 percent of the time slot to leave room.

### Rehearse out loud

The calculator gives you a word-based estimate. Rehearsing aloud with a timer tells you how long it really takes. Use both: the calculator for drafting, rehearsal for final timing.

### Write for the ear

Spoken scripts work best with shorter sentences, simple words and clear signposting. Long, complex sentences are hard to say and hard to follow. Check sentence length and other metrics with [Text Statistics](/tools/text-statistics).

### Mark your pauses

Add notes for planned pauses, demos or questions. These take time the word count cannot see.

## Common mistakes

- **Using reading speed for speech.** Silent reading is much faster than speaking. For written articles, use the [Reading Time Calculator](/tools/reading-time) instead.
- **Speeding up under pressure.** Nerves make many speakers rush. Plan for your calm pace, not your nervous one.
- **Counting stage directions.** Remove notes like "next slide" or "pause here" before calculating, or they will inflate the estimate.

## FAQ

### How long is a 1,000-word speech?

At a comfortable 130 words per minute, a 1,000-word speech runs about 7 minutes 42 seconds. At a slower 100 WPM it takes about 10 minutes.

### Is speaking time the same for video voiceovers?

Voiceovers follow the same formula, but many narrators speak a little faster than a live presenter. Time a short sample and adjust the pace setting to match.

### How do I make my speech shorter without losing content?

Cut repeated points, replace long examples with a single sharp one, and move supporting detail to handouts or slides. Shorter sentences also help you speak at a steadier pace.

### Does the calculator account for pauses?

No. It estimates time from word count and your chosen pace only. Add time manually for pauses, questions and transitions.
