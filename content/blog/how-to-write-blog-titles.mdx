To write blog titles that get clicks, state a clear benefit, put your main keyword near the start, and keep the title roughly 50 to 60 characters so it is less likely to be cut off in search results. Proven formulas such as "How to," "Why," numbered lists and "Complete Guide" work because they set clear expectations. Write several options, then pick the one that is most specific and honest about what the post delivers.

A blog title has two audiences: the searcher scanning a results page and the search engine trying to understand your topic. A great title satisfies both. It tells people exactly what they will get and gives them a reason to choose your result over the others.

## Why your blog title matters so much

The title is often the only part of your post someone sees before deciding whether to click. In search results, on social feeds and in newsletters, it does the job of a shop window. A strong post with a vague title can be overlooked, while a clear, specific title can lift attention even for a modest post.

Titles also anchor your SEO. The title tag is one of the clearest relevance signals on a page, which is why most SEO guides recommend placing the primary keyword near the beginning. Our [SEO basics guide](/blog/seo-basics) covers how titles fit into the bigger picture.

## Proven blog headline formulas

| Formula | Example | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How to [achieve result] | How to Start a Podcast on a Small Budget | Step-by-step tutorials |
| [Number] [Things] for [Result] | 7 Email Subject Lines That Get Opened | List posts and roundups |
| Why [Topic] Matters | Why Page Speed Matters for Small Shops | Opinion and explainer posts |
| The Complete Guide to [Topic] | The Complete Guide to Local SEO | Long, comprehensive pillar pages |
| [Topic] Explained | Core Web Vitals Explained | Beginner-friendly explainers |
| [Topic] for Beginners | Composting for Beginners | Audience-targeted intros |

The formula is only the frame. The specifics, such as the audience, the result and the constraint, make it compelling.

## Best practices for writing blog titles

**Lead with the keyword.** Front-loading the main phrase helps both scanning readers and search engines.

**Be specific.** "How to Cut Your Grocery Bill" is good. "How to Cut Your Grocery Bill by Meal Planning on Sundays" is better because it promises a method.

**Stay within length.** Around 50 to 60 characters is a commonly recommended range for search titles. Preview yours with the [SERP Preview](/tools/serp-preview) tool if you want to see how it might display.

**Match the content.** A title that overpromises might win a click, but readers who leave quickly are not a win.

**Use numbers honestly.** Odd or specific numbers can stand out, but only if they reflect the post.

**Write ten, keep one.** Your first idea is rarely your best. Generate options and compare them side by side.

## Common blog title mistakes

- **Clickbait.** "You Won't Believe..." titles erode trust when the content does not deliver.
- **Vague wording.** "Some Thoughts on Marketing" gives no reason to click.
- **Keyword stuffing.** "SEO Tips SEO Guide SEO Strategy" reads like spam.
- **Ignoring the date.** A year in the title signals freshness, but it must be updated or removed when the post ages.
- **Same title and H1 everywhere.** It is fine for them to match, but you can also use a slightly longer H1 on the page and a tighter title tag.

## FAQ

### Should my blog title and SEO title be the same?

They can be, and often are. Some writers use a longer, more descriptive headline on the page and a shorter title tag tuned for search results. Both should describe the same content.

### Are questions good blog titles?

Questions can work well when they match how people search, such as "What Is Keyword Density?" Make sure the post answers the question early.

### Should I put the year in my blog title?

Only for content where freshness matters, such as tools, trends or annual guides, and only if you plan to update it. An outdated year can discourage clicks.

### How do I test which blog title works best?

Compare click-through rates in your search console data after publishing, or test variations in email subject lines and social posts. Change one variable at a time so you can learn what made the difference.
