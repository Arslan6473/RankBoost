## What counts as a character

A character is any single symbol in your text. That includes:

- Letters, uppercase and lowercase
- Digits from 0 to 9
- Spaces, tabs and line breaks
- Punctuation and symbols such as commas, periods, hyphens, quotes and the at sign

Because spaces and line breaks are characters too, there are two numbers that matter:

- **Characters with spaces** is the full length of the text. Most platforms use this figure for their limits.
- **Characters without spaces** strips out all whitespace. Some publishers, translators and academic forms use this figure instead.

Always check which version a platform or brief means before you trim.

## Why character count matters

Many places where your text appears have a fixed amount of room. Go over, and the platform either rejects the text or cuts it off.

- **Search results:** Long title tags and meta descriptions are often truncated in search results. Keeping them within commonly recommended ranges helps your full message show.
- **Social media:** Posts on X (formerly Twitter) allow up to 280 characters for standard accounts.
- **SMS:** A single standard text message holds 160 characters using the basic character set; longer messages are split into multiple parts.
- **Forms and apps:** Usernames, bios, ad headlines and push notifications all have their own caps.

## Common character limits

These are widely used guidelines. Platforms change their rules from time to time, so confirm limits for anything business-critical.

| Where | Commonly cited limit |
| --- | --- |
| SEO title tag | About 50 to 60 characters |
| Meta description | About 150 to 160 characters |
| Post on X | 280 characters |
| Standard SMS segment | 160 characters |
| URL slug | Short and readable; no fixed limit |

Search engines actually truncate titles and descriptions by pixel width, not by a strict character count, so a title full of wide letters may cut off sooner. Character ranges remain a practical proxy.

## Best practices for staying under a character limit

### Lead with what matters

Put the most important words first. If a title or description does get truncated, the reader still sees the core message.

### Cut without losing meaning

- Swap long phrases for short ones: "use" instead of "make use of".
- Remove filler words such as "really", "very" and "just".
- Use numerals instead of spelled-out numbers where style allows.
- Drop redundant punctuation and double spaces.

### Watch invisible characters

Extra spaces, trailing line breaks and tabs all count. If your total looks higher than expected, compare the with-spaces and without-spaces figures. A big gap often means stray whitespace or extra line breaks, and the [Line Counter](/tools/line-counter) can show how many lines your text really contains.

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing words and characters.** A 160-character limit is very different from a 160-word limit. If you need a word total, use the [Word Counter](/tools/word-counter).
- **Forgetting emoji and special symbols.** Some platforms count certain emoji as more than one character, so leave a small buffer.
- **Counting in a different editor.** Rich text editors can add hidden formatting. Paste plain text to get an accurate number.

## FAQ

### Do line breaks count as characters?

Yes. A line break is a character, and most platforms include it in their total. The RankBoost tool counts it as whitespace, so it shows up in the spaces figure and is excluded from the no-spaces total.

### How many characters is a typical sentence?

It varies widely, but a 15 to 20 word sentence in English usually lands somewhere around 80 to 120 characters including spaces. Use the counter to check your own writing rather than relying on an average.

### Why is my meta description cut off even though it is under 160 characters?

Search engines truncate snippets based on display width and device, and sometimes rewrite descriptions entirely. Keeping the key message near the start is the most reliable defense.

### Is character count the same in every language?

No. Different scripts use characters differently, and some platforms weight certain characters more heavily. Check the specific platform's rules for non-Latin text.
