To simplify complex words, find the long, multi-syllable words in your draft and replace them with shorter, everyday alternatives that mean the same thing, such as "use" instead of "utilize" or "help" instead of "facilitate". Keep a complex word only when there's no simpler word that's just as accurate. As a rough guide, try to keep complex words under about 15% of your total.

The free [Complex Word Finder](/tools/complex-word-finder) makes the first step easy. Paste your text and it lists the words with three or more syllables, along with the share of complex words in your draft.

## What counts as a complex word?

In readability formulas, a "complex word" usually means a word with three or more syllables. This is the definition used by the Gunning Fog index, and it's the one the RankBoost tool uses too.

Not every complex word is a problem. "Information", "community" and "important" are long but familiar. The words worth targeting are the ones that are long and formal when a short, common word would do.

## Why simpler words improve readability

- **They're faster to read.** Short, familiar words are recognized almost instantly.
- **They lower your reading grade level.** Formulas such as Flesch-Kincaid, Gunning Fog and SMOG all rise as syllable counts rise.
- **They make writing more inclusive.** Plain words help non-native speakers and readers skimming on small screens.
- **They sound more confident.** Inflated vocabulary can come across as padding.

## Simple words instead of complex words

Here are common swaps that keep the meaning intact:

| Complex word | Simpler alternative |
| --- | --- |
| utilize | use |
| facilitate | help, make easier |
| approximately | about |
| demonstrate | show |
| subsequently | later, then |
| commence | start |
| additional | more, extra |
| sufficient | enough |
| individuals | people |
| methodology | method |
| prioritize | focus on |
| optimization | improvement |

Some of these, like "commence", are only two syllables but are still more formal than needed. Use the tool to find long words, then use your judgment for formal ones.

## When to keep a complex word

Plain language doesn't mean removing every long word. Keep a complex word when:

- **It's the correct technical term.** On an SEO page, "canonicalization" may be exactly the word your readers search for. Explain it once, then use it consistently.
- **It's a name or brand.** Product names, places and people stay as they are.
- **The simple version changes the meaning.** "Approximately" and "about" are interchangeable; "regulation" and "rule" may not be in a legal context.
- **It's already familiar.** Everyday words like "everyone" or "different" don't need replacing.

## Other ways to reduce word complexity

### Rewrite nominalizations

Nominalizations turn verbs into nouns, which adds syllables. "Make a determination" becomes "decide". "Conduct an evaluation" becomes "evaluate", or simply "check".

### Break up compound jargon

Phrases like "customer engagement optimization strategy" stack several long words together. Say what you mean: "ways to get customers more involved".

### Explain acronyms and terms once

If you need a specialist term, define it in plain words the first time it appears. Readers will then follow it easily.

## Common mistakes

- **Replacing words with less precise ones.** Simplify the wording, not the accuracy.
- **Swapping one long word for a long phrase.** "Facilitate" to "make it possible for" doesn't help much. "Help" does.
- **Targeting 0%.** Some complex words are unavoidable. Aim for a reasonable share, not none.
- **Ignoring sentence length.** Simple words in a 40-word sentence still make for hard reading. Check sentence length with the [Long Sentence Detector](/tools/long-sentence-detector).

## FAQ

### Is a two-syllable word ever complex?

For readability formulas, no; they usually count three or more syllables. In practice, short but unusual words such as "ergo" can still confuse readers, so read your draft with your audience in mind.

### Will simplifying words lower my reading grade?

Usually, yes. Most grade-level formulas factor in syllables or word length, so replacing long words tends to lower the grade. Confirm the change with the [Reading Grade Level](/tools/reading-grade) tool.

### Should I simplify words in technical documentation?

Simplify the surrounding language, but keep the precise technical terms your readers expect. Clear explanations around necessary jargon are more helpful than vague substitutes.

### How many complex words is too many?

Around 15% or less is a reasonable target for general web content. Technical and academic writing often runs higher, which is fine if the audience expects it.
