## Title case vs sentence case

These are the two styles writers debate most.

**Title Case** capitalizes the main words and keeps short words such as "of", "the", "and" and "to" lowercase (unless they start the title). It signals "this is a headline" and is traditional for article titles and many style guides.

**Sentence case** capitalizes only the first word of each sentence, just like normal prose. Many modern brands and publications prefer it for headings because it feels conversational and is easier to read at a glance.

Neither is better for SEO. Search engines don't rank one case style over another. What matters is consistency across your site and a title that reads well in search results. For more on writing strong titles, see our [on-page SEO guide](/blog/on-page-seo-guide).

## Gotchas to check before publishing

### Acronyms and proper nouns

Title Case and Sentence case both start by lowercasing your text, then capitalizing where the rules say to. That means acronyms like "SEO" or "HTML" can come out as "Seo" or "html", and names like "iPhone" or "McDonald" lose their internal capitals. Sentence case also lowercases proper nouns and the word "I" in the middle of a sentence. Always give the output a quick read and fix those by hand.

### Small words after punctuation

In Title Case, small words are capitalized only when they're the very first word of your input, so convert one title at a time for best results. A small word right after a colon, as in "Writing Tips: the Basics", stays lowercase. Some style guides capitalize the word after a colon, so adjust if your guide does.

### Developer cases drop special characters

camelCase, snake_case and kebab-case keep only plain letters and numbers. Punctuation and spaces become word boundaries, and accented letters such as "é" are removed rather than converted. For clean, URL-friendly slugs, the [Slug Generator](/tools/slug-generator) is purpose-built for the job.

### Alternating case counts every character

aLtErNaTiNg case switches on every character, spaces and punctuation included, so the pattern may not restart neatly at each word. That's fine for a joke, but don't use it anywhere readability matters.

## Best practices for consistent capitalization

- **Pick one heading style** for your site and apply it everywhere: page titles, H2s, navigation and buttons.
- **Avoid long passages in all caps.** Uppercase is harder to read in large blocks and can come across as shouting.
- **Use lowercase to normalize data** before comparing or deduplicating lists, so "Apple" and "apple" match.
- **Clean spacing first.** If your text came from a PDF or a messy paste, run it through [Remove Extra Spaces](/tools/remove-extra-spaces) before converting, especially for snake_case and kebab-case.

## FAQ

### How do I fix text typed with caps lock on?

Paste it into the Case Converter and choose Sentence case. Everything is lowercased and the first letter of each sentence is capitalized. Then restore capitals on names and acronyms.

### Should meta titles use Title Case?

It's a style choice. Title Case can make a result stand out, while Sentence case often reads more naturally. Either works as long as you're consistent and stay within commonly recommended title lengths of around 50 to 60 characters.

### What's the difference between camelCase and PascalCase?

camelCase starts with a lowercase letter, like "pageTitle". PascalCase capitalizes the first letter too, like "PageTitle". The tool produces camelCase; for PascalCase, capitalize the first letter of the result.
