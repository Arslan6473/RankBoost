To count word frequency, you split a text into individual words, treat uppercase and lowercase versions as the same word, and tally how many times each one appears. Sorting that tally from most to least common gives you a word frequency table, which instantly shows the words you rely on most. A frequency analyzer does the counting for you and adds each word's share of the total.

This guide shows how to count word frequency with the free [Keyword Frequency Analyzer](/tools/keyword-frequency-analyzer) and how to use the results to edit more effectively.

## What a word frequency count is

A word frequency count is a complete inventory of the words in a text and how often each one occurs. Unlike tools that filter out filler, a full frequency count includes every word, from "the" and "and" to your most specialized terms.

That completeness is the point. It lets you see your writing habits as they really are, including the small words that quietly pad sentences.

## Word frequency vs keyword density

These two ideas are related but answer different questions:

| Aspect | Word frequency count | Keyword density |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting point | No keyword needed | You check specific terms |
| What it covers | Every word in the text | Chosen words or phrases |
| Main output | Ranked table of all words | Percentage for each term |
| Best for | Editing habits, overuse, repetition | Checking focus on a target keyword |

If you want to measure a target phrase, read our guide to [keyword density](/blog/what-is-keyword-density). If you want to see the full picture of your vocabulary, a frequency count is the better tool.

## Why counting word frequency is useful

- **Find overused words.** Many writers have pet words, like "really", "just" or "very", that show up far more than they realize.
- **Catch repetitive phrasing.** If a distinctive word appears many times in a short piece, readers will notice.
- **Check vocabulary balance.** A text dominated by a handful of words can feel monotonous.
- **Compare drafts.** Run before-and-after versions to confirm your edits reduced repetition.
- **Review transcripts and survey responses.** Frequent words in open-ended feedback hint at recurring themes.

## How to count word frequency step by step

### Step 1: Paste your text

Open the [Keyword Frequency Analyzer](/tools/keyword-frequency-analyzer) and paste your content. It works on anything from a single paragraph to a full article.

### Step 2: Check the summary metrics

At the top you'll see total words, the number of unique words, and the most frequent word with its count. A low ratio of unique words to total words suggests heavy repetition.

### Step 3: Read the frequency table

The frequency table lists the 20 most common words, each with its count and percentage of the total. A bar chart shows the top 10 so you can see at a glance how steeply usage drops off.

Counting is case-insensitive, so "SEO" and "seo" are tallied together. Hyphenated words such as "long-term" and contractions such as "don't" each count as a single word.

### Step 4: Separate filler from content words

Expect words like "the", "and" and "to" near the top; they're common in all English writing. Look past them to the first content words. Those are your dominant themes, or your overused ones.

### Step 5: Edit and re-run

Swap repetitive words for precise alternatives, restructure sentences, or cut redundant ones. Then paste the revised text and compare.

## Example: reading a frequency table

Take this sample:

> SEO is important. SEO helps websites rank. Good SEO combines content, links, and technical health. Learn SEO to grow your traffic.

| Word | Count | Share |
| --- | --- | --- |
| seo | 4 | 19% |
| and | 1 | 4.8% |
| combines | 1 | 4.8% |
| content | 1 | 4.8% |

With 21 words in total, "seo" makes up nearly a fifth of the text. In a four-sentence snippet that's expected, but in a full article a single content word holding that kind of share would read as forced and repetitive.

## Tips for acting on your results

- **Look for unexpected words.** Your main topic should be near the top. Surprising entries usually point to habits worth fixing.
- **Vary, don't avoid.** Replacing every repeat with a synonym can make writing feel strained. Use pronouns, restructure sentences, or simply cut.

## FAQ

### What counts as a word in a frequency count?

Here, a word is a run of letters, numbers and apostrophes, optionally joined by hyphens. Punctuation is ignored, so "content," and "content" count as the same word.

### Why do small words dominate the table?

Function words like "the", "of" and "and" are the most common words in English, so they top almost every frequency table. Focus on the first content words below them.

### Is a high frequency for my main keyword a problem?

Not by itself. Your main topic should be among your most frequent content words. It becomes a problem when it crowds out natural variation and makes sentences read awkwardly.

### Can I use a word frequency count for non-SEO writing?

Yes. Essays, reports, speeches and fiction all benefit from spotting overused words. The same table works regardless of what you're writing.
