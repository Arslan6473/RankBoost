To write a meta description, summarise the page in one or two sentences, include your primary keyword naturally, highlight a clear benefit and end with a call to action — all in roughly 120–160 characters. It won't directly boost rankings, but a well-written meta description can make your result more appealing than the ones around it. ## What a meta description is

A meta description is an HTML tag that summarises a page's content. It sits in the head section of the page and looks like this:

```html
<meta name="description" content="Discover running shoes with free shipping and easy returns. Shop now." />
```

Search engines often show it as the grey text beneath your title in search results. Google may also choose to show a different snippet pulled from your page content if it thinks that better answers a particular query, so your description is a strong suggestion rather than a guarantee.

## Why meta descriptions matter

Meta descriptions are generally not treated as a direct ranking factor. Their value is in persuasion. When someone scans a page of results, the description helps them decide which listing best matches what they want. A specific, relevant description can improve your click-through rate, and more clicks from the same ranking position means more traffic.

When a searcher's query terms appear in your description, Google typically highlights them in bold, which can make your result stand out further.

## Meta description length

The commonly recommended range is around **150–160 characters**, with many SEOs aiming for 120–155 to stay safely inside the display limit. Google truncates by pixel width rather than a fixed character count, and mobile results can show a different amount of text than desktop.

Practical guidance:

- Aim for roughly 120–160 characters.
- Put the most important information in the first half.
- Don't pad a description just to hit a number. Clear and complete beats long.

## Meta description best practices

### Write a unique description for every page

Duplicate descriptions across many pages make them look interchangeable in search results. Prioritise unique descriptions for your most important pages first: the homepage, key category and product pages, and top-performing articles.

### Include the keyword naturally

Use your primary keyword once, in a way that reads well. Searchers are reassured when they see the term they typed.

### Lead with value

Tell people what they'll get: an answer, a product, a free tool, a comparison. Specifics like "free returns" or "10 examples" tend to be more persuasive than generic promises.

### Use an action-oriented CTA

Ending with a short call to action, such as "Learn more" or "Shop now," gives the description a clear purpose. Choose a CTA that matches intent: transactional pages suit "Shop now," while guides suit "Read the guide."

### Avoid quotation marks that break the tag

Double quotation marks inside the content attribute can cut your description short in the HTML. Use single quotes or rephrase.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Keyword stuffing.** Repeating the keyword several times reads poorly and doesn't help.
- **Mismatching the page.** A description that promises something the page doesn't offer leads to quick bounces.
- **Leaving it blank.** Without one, Google picks text from your page, which may not be your best pitch.
- **Burying the point.** If the key benefit appears after 160 characters, most searchers won't see it.
- **Writing for every query at once.** Focus on the main intent of the page.

## FAQ

### Can a meta description be too short?

Yes. A very short description, such as five or six words, wastes space you could use to persuade. It also gives Google more reason to replace it with text from your page.

### Should every page have a meta description?

Ideally, yes, but prioritise. Important landing pages benefit most. For large sites with thousands of similar pages, a carefully templated description is better than none.

### Why is Google showing a different description from the one I wrote?

Google may generate its own snippet when it thinks other text on your page better matches a specific query. Keeping your description accurate and relevant to the page's main topic makes it more likely to be used.

### Should I use emojis or special characters?

They can occasionally help a result stand out, but they don't always display and can look unprofessional in some industries. Use them sparingly, if at all.
