## What a good summary does

A useful summary isn't just a shorter version of the text. It:

- **Captures the main point** in the first sentence.
- **Keeps the key facts** such as names, numbers and decisions.
- **Drops detail** like examples, asides and repetition.
- **Stays neutral**, without adding opinions the original didn't contain.

Summaries are handy for writing a TL;DR at the top of a long post, briefing a colleague, building meta descriptions and excerpts, or getting the gist of research before deciding whether to read it in full.

## Choosing the right summary length

| Use case | Suggested length |
| --- | --- |
| TL;DR at the top of a blog post | Short |
| Social media caption | Short |
| Blog excerpt or newsletter blurb | Medium |
| Briefing a teammate | Medium |
| Executive summary of a report | Long |
| Study notes | Long |

If you're turning a summary into a meta description, aim for roughly 150 to 160 characters, the commonly recommended range for search snippets. A short summary is usually the best starting point.

## How to prepare text for a better summary

### Remove clutter first

Navigation menus, cookie notices, ads and footers can sneak into copied web pages. Delete them before summarizing so they don't distract from the real content.

### Summarize long documents in sections

For very long reports, summarize one section at a time, then combine the section summaries. This makes it easier to check accuracy and keeps important points from being dropped.

### Keep headings in place

Headings help show how the original is organized. Leaving them in your input can help you check the summary covers each part.

## How to check an AI summary for accuracy

AI summaries can omit important details or phrase things more strongly than the source does. Always review before you publish or share. Check that:

- **Facts match the original.** Numbers, names, dates and conclusions should be exactly as stated.
- **Nothing critical is missing.** If the original has a key caveat or limitation, the summary should reflect it.
- **No new claims were added.** The summary should only contain information from the source.
- **The tone is neutral.** Watch for wording that sounds more certain or more dramatic than the original.

## Common mistakes

- **Summarizing a fragment.** Pasting only the first few paragraphs gives you a summary of the introduction, not the whole piece.
- **Publishing without reviewing.** Treat the output as a draft, especially for anything public or high-stakes.
- **Using the wrong length.** A one-sentence summary of a 5,000-word report will inevitably leave out a lot. Pick the length to match the job.
- **Summarizing confidential documents carelessly.** Check your organization's policy before pasting sensitive material into any online AI tool.

## FAQ

### Is an AI summary the same as paraphrasing?

No. Paraphrasing rewrites text at roughly the same length using different words. Summarizing shortens it to the main points. Use a summarizer to condense and a rewriter to rephrase.

### Can I use an AI summary in my own article?

You can use it as a starting point, but rewrite it in your own words and credit the original source where appropriate. Copying a summary of someone else's work without attribution can still raise plagiarism concerns.

### How long should my input be?

There's no fixed minimum, but summaries work best on complete pieces of several paragraphs or more. Very short inputs leave little to condense.

### Why does the summary miss a point I think is important?

Shorter summaries have to leave things out. Try the medium or long option, or add the missing point yourself when you edit.
