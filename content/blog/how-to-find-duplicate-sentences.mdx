## Why duplicate sentences happen

Repeated sentences are rarely deliberate. They usually creep in through the editing process:

- **Merging drafts** from several writers or several versions of the same file
- **Copy and paste** mistakes when moving sections around
- **Templates and boilerplate** such as disclaimers or calls to action pasted into multiple places
- **Exports and imports** from content systems or document converters that duplicate blocks
- **Late edits** where a sentence is rewritten but the old version is not deleted

Because each copy usually sits far apart, they are easy to miss when reading start to finish.

## Why it matters

A sentence that appears twice makes a document look unedited. Readers notice, and it can undermine trust in otherwise solid content. Duplicated text also adds length without adding value, which is the opposite of what you want in helpful content. On web pages, repeated boilerplate can also make pages look more alike than they really are.

## How matching works

Knowing how the comparison works helps you interpret results:

- **Case is ignored.** "Contact us today." and "contact us today." are treated as the same sentence.
- **Spacing is normalized.** Extra spaces, tabs or line breaks inside a sentence do not stop a match.
- **Wording must otherwise match.** A changed word, an added comma or different end punctuation, such as a period versus an exclamation mark, makes two sentences count as different.
- **Sentences are split at end punctuation.** Periods, question marks and exclamation points mark the end of a sentence.

Because the match is exact apart from case and spacing, the tool finds true duplicates rather than sentences that merely say something similar. Listed sentences are shown in lowercase, so use your editor's search to locate each copy in the original document.

## Best practices for cleaning up duplicates

### Decide which copy to keep

The first instance is not always the right one. Keep the version in the place where it does the most work, usually where the idea is introduced or best supported.

### Check intentional repetition

Some repetition is deliberate: a repeated call to action, a refrain in a speech, or a legal notice required in more than one place. Leave those in and remove only the accidental copies.

### Look for near-duplicates too

Sentences that differ by a single word are not flagged, but they often come from the same editing mistake. While you are fixing exact duplicates, skim nearby paragraphs for close variations.

### Clean line-based lists separately

If your text is a list of items, one per line, rather than prose, line-based deduplication is a better fit. The [Remove Duplicate Lines](/tools/remove-duplicate-lines) tool handles that case.

### Recheck after editing

Paste the cleaned text in again to confirm the duplicate count is zero.

## Common mistakes

- **Only checking part of the document.** Duplicates are often far apart, so paste the full text.
- **Missing repeated words.** A document can be free of duplicate sentences but still lean heavily on a few words. Check with the [Duplicate Word Finder](/tools/duplicate-word-finder).
- **Pasting headings and lists.** Text without end punctuation can merge into the next sentence and hide a duplicate. Paste body text for the cleanest results.

## FAQ

### Can the tool find duplicate paragraphs?

It works at the sentence level. A duplicated paragraph shows up as several duplicated sentences, which makes it easy to spot and remove.

### Does it detect plagiarism?

No. It compares sentences within the single text you paste. It does not check your text against other documents or the web.

### Will it find sentences that are rephrased?

No. Sentences must match apart from capitalization and spacing. Reworded sentences with the same meaning are treated as different.

### Is there a document size limit?

Because the tool runs in your browser, there is no server-side limit. Very large documents may take a moment longer depending on your device.
