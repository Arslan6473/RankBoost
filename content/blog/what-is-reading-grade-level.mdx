Reading grade level is an estimate of the US school grade a person needs to have completed to comfortably understand a piece of text. A grade level of 8, for example, means the writing should be readable by a typical 13- to 14-year-old. Most web content works best at roughly grade 6 to 8.

You can find the reading grade level of any draft with the free [Reading Grade Level tool](/tools/reading-grade). It combines several established readability formulas into a single consensus grade, so you don't have to pick one formula and hope it's right.

## Why reading grade level matters

Grade level isn't a measure of how smart your readers are. Even highly educated people prefer text that's easy to skim, especially on a phone or when they're short on time. Writing at a lower grade level helps you:

- **Reach more people**, including non-native speakers and readers who are tired or distracted.
- **Get to the point faster**, because simpler sentences leave less room for padding.
- **Reduce misunderstandings** in instructions, policies and support content.

## How reading grade level is calculated

Grade-level formulas count surface features of text and convert them into a school grade. Different formulas emphasize different features:

| Formula | What it looks at |
| --- | --- |
| Flesch-Kincaid Grade | Words per sentence and syllables per word |
| Gunning Fog | Words per sentence and share of words with 3+ syllables |
| SMOG | Number of words with 3+ syllables relative to sentences |
| Coleman-Liau | Letters per word and sentences per word |
| ARI | Characters per word and words per sentence |

Because each formula has its own blind spots, the RankBoost tool averages the grade-level results to produce one balanced figure. As a rough example, a draft that averages 15 words per sentence and 1.5 syllables per word comes out near grade 8 on Flesch-Kincaid.

## What reading level should you write at?

There's no single correct answer, but these ranges are a reasonable starting point:

| Content type | Suggested grade level |
| --- | --- |
| Consumer marketing, landing pages | 5–7 |
| Blog posts and general web content | 6–8 |
| News and magazine features | 8–10 |
| B2B guides and white papers | 9–12 |
| Academic and specialist writing | 12+ |

The RankBoost tool reflects this with color coding: grade 8 or below shows as green, grades 9 to 12 as amber, and anything above 12 as red. It also labels the result with a school band, from Elementary school through Middle school, High school, College and Postgraduate.

## How to lower your reading grade level

### Shorten sentences

Every grade-level formula rewards shorter sentences. Aim for an average of around 15 to 20 words and break up anything that runs much longer. The [Long Sentence Detector](/tools/long-sentence-detector) lists the worst offenders first.

### Choose shorter, familiar words

Long, multi-syllable words push the grade up quickly. Replace them where a plain word works just as well:

| Instead of | Try |
| --- | --- |
| utilize | use |
| approximately | about |
| demonstrate | show |
| subsequently | later |
| facilitate | help |

The [Complex Word Finder](/tools/complex-word-finder) highlights every word with three or more syllables so you can review them in one pass.

### Put one idea in each sentence

If a sentence has two "and" clauses and a "which", it probably holds more than one idea. Split it.

### Use lists and subheadings

Breaking a long paragraph into a short list makes the content easier to scan. Note that readability formulas work best on full sentences, so score your prose rather than your bullet points.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the grade as a hard rule.** It's an estimate. A grade 9 result on a technical page may be perfectly fine.
- **Dumbing down the content.** Lower the grade by simplifying how you say things, not by removing important information.
- **Relying on one formula.** Individual formulas can disagree by several grades. A consensus view is more reliable.
- **Ignoring necessary terms.** If your readers need a term like "canonical URL", keep it and explain it once.

## FAQ

### Is a lower reading grade level always better?

No. Very low grades can make writing feel choppy or patronizing. Aim for the lowest level that still communicates your ideas accurately for your audience.

### Why does the grade change when I edit only one paragraph?

Grade-level formulas are based on averages. A single very long sentence or a cluster of complex words can shift the overall figure, especially in shorter texts.

### Do reading grade levels apply outside the US?

The scale is based on US school grades, but the underlying idea works anywhere. Treat grade 8 as roughly the reading level of a typical early teenager.

### Can headings and lists skew the result?

Yes. Headings and bullet points often lack end punctuation, which can make sentences look longer than they are. Check your main body text for the most accurate grade.
