Search intent is the goal behind a search query: what the person actually wants to find, learn or do when they type it. It's usually grouped into four types: informational (learn something), navigational (reach a specific site), commercial (compare options before buying) and transactional (complete a purchase or action). Matching your content to the intent of a keyword is one of the most important steps in ranking for it.

This guide explains each type of search intent, how to identify it, and how to classify a keyword list quickly with the free [Search Intent Analyzer](/tools/search-intent-analyzer).

## Why search intent matters

Search engines aim to show results that satisfy the searcher. If someone searches "how to clean running shoes", they want instructions, not a product page. If they search "buy running shoes online", a tutorial won't help them.

That means a page can be well written and well optimized yet still struggle to rank if it's the wrong type of page for the query. Understanding intent helps you:

- Choose the right content format before you write
- Avoid targeting keywords your page can't satisfy
- Map keywords to stages of the marketing funnel
- Write titles and descriptions that match what searchers expect

## The four types of search intent

### Informational intent

The searcher wants to learn something. These queries often include words like "how", "what", "why", "guide", "tutorial", "tips", "ideas", "examples" or "meaning". Best formats: guides, how-to articles, explainers and glossaries.

### Navigational intent

The searcher wants to reach a particular site or page. Queries may include "login", "sign in", "official", "website" or a domain name. Best formats: your own homepage, login and key brand pages. It's hard to rank for another brand's navigational queries, and usually not worth trying.

### Commercial intent

Sometimes called commercial investigation, this is research before a purchase. Signal words include "best", "top", "review", "vs", "compare", "comparison" and "alternative". Best formats: comparison posts, reviews, roundups and buyer's guides.

### Transactional intent

The searcher is ready to act. Look for "buy", "order", "purchase", "price", "cheap", "deal", "coupon", "discount", "for sale" or "shipping". Best formats: product pages, category pages, pricing pages and sign-up pages.

## How to identify search intent

### Look for signal words

The words in a query are the quickest clue, which is exactly how keyword intent tools work. "Best" suggests comparison, "buy" suggests a transaction.

### Check the search results

The most reliable method is to search the keyword yourself and look at what ranks. If the top results are all listicles, Google has decided the intent is commercial or informational, whatever the words suggest. If you see shopping results, intent leans transactional.

### Consider mixed intent

Some queries serve more than one purpose. "Running shoes" could mean browsing products or learning about types. In these cases, results usually show a mix, and a page that serves the dominant intent while acknowledging the other can work well.

## Common mistakes with keyword intent

- **Forcing a product page onto an informational query.** Write the guide, then link to products from it.
- **Relying on signal words alone.** They're a fast first pass. Always confirm important keywords against the actual results.
- **Ignoring intent shifts.** Intent can change over time, for example around product launches or seasons. Recheck key terms periodically.
- **Mixing intents in one page.** A page that tries to be a tutorial, a review and a checkout at once usually satisfies none of them.

## FAQ

### Can a single keyword have more than one intent?

Yes. Broad queries often have mixed intent. Look at the search results to see which intent Google favors, and build your page around that while covering the secondary need briefly.

### Does search intent affect how I write the title?

It should. Informational titles often promise an explanation or steps, commercial titles signal comparison or rankings, and transactional titles highlight price, offers or availability.

### How do I find intent for keywords with no clear signal words?

Search them and study the top results. The formats that rank, such as guides, product pages or videos, tell you what searchers expect.

### Should every page target only one intent?

Generally, yes. One primary intent per page keeps the content focused. Supporting sections can address related questions without changing the page's main purpose.
